Coverage: 30 October–6 November 2026; selected works were posted 2–3 November.

Scope: civilian multirotor control, degraded-condition localization, repeated inspection and low-bandwidth multi-UAV coordination.

## Takeaway

This week's strongest engineering theme is **preserving reliable behavior when sensing, timing or environment continuity breaks**. One paper certifies multirotor control allocation under uncertain command delays; another explores magnetic-inertial localization when cameras fail; a third registers repeated visual surveys despite large scene changes; and a conference paper demonstrates formation path following with minimal inter-agent communication.

The [previous brief](2026-10-30.md), the current [phone recorder proposal](../drone-atlas/phone-mvp.md), and the live repository tree were reviewed. The repository contains no AGENTS.md. No working AER recorder, SITL integration, magnetometer array or multi-aircraft platform is assumed. Previous selections are not repeated.

## 1. Delay-certified control allocation for overactuated multirotors

[Paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03490) — posted **2 November 2026**, v1.  
Status: arXiv preprint submitted to IEEE; acceptance and independent replication were not verified.

**Demonstrated/author report:** the method protects a minimum “readiness” level—the ability of an overactuated vehicle to change its commanded wrench—despite sampled commands and uncertain rotor-speed-controller delays. It computes a reachable rotor-speed box and tests its worst relevant corner, then applies a fallback torque if needed. In hexarotor simulation with 2–40 ms delay and ±2 ms jitter, the filter reportedly never crossed the readiness floor. Outside the design envelope, a comparison predictor violated the floor in 2–16 of 16 missions, while the proposed filter traded 2–30% additional tracking error for maintaining the constraint.

**Why it matters for AER:** it extends last week's controller-comparison lesson: timing is not only a performance statistic; it can be part of the safety state. AER should record delay distributions and define fallback behavior before optimizing tracking error.

**Limits/resources:** evidence is simulation-only and specific to overactuated multirotors with structured rotor dynamics. The method is not a general guarantee for ordinary quadrotors, estimator failures or perception delays. No official runnable code, PX4/ArduPilot integration, dataset or tested embedded hardware was linked from the primary record.

## 2. MISLAM — magnetic-inertial localization when vision degrades

[Paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03106) — posted **2 November 2026**, v1.  
Status: arXiv preprint; peer review and independent replication were not verified.

**Demonstrated/author report:** MISLAM tightly couples an IMU with a nine-magnetometer array, local polynomial magnetic-field models and a global Gaussian-process map in an error-state Kalman filter. Offline evaluation used three indoor datasets collected with a handheld rig. The authors report 45.7–78.0% reductions in endpoint closure error relative to magnetic-inertial odometry. In poor illumination, it continued after visual-inertial odometry failed and reported 0.332 m horizontal closure error; vertical drift remained a stated limitation.

**Why it matters for AER:** magnetic fields are usually treated only as heading inputs or interference sources. This work suggests testing whether repeatable local magnetic structure can act as a complementary localization channel in visually degraded civilian inspection environments.

**Limits/resources:** this is handheld offline evaluation, not aerial flight. Nine magnetometers add calibration, synchronization, mass, wiring and electromagnetic-interference challenges. Magnetic maps can change with equipment and current flow; vertical performance remains weak. No official code, data release or hardware bill was verified from the arXiv record.

## 3. Sparse anchors for repeated visual surveys

[Paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05084) — posted **3 November 2026**, v1.  
Status: arXiv preprint; peer review and independent replication were not verified.

**Demonstrated/author report:** the method registers a new session to an older reference map using a small set of geometrically verified absolute pose anchors plus within-session relative poses. Sequence consensus rejects inconsistent alignment hypotheses; featuremetric refinement and robust pose-graph optimization complete the trajectory. On 15 cross-session experiments from the HILTI26 construction-site dataset, the authors report 94.4% recall within 1 m and 100% within 5 m, versus independent-image localization peaking at 65.3% and 70.7%.

**Why it matters for AER:** repeated inspection is more realistic than assuming a permanent, unchanged map. The architecture separates sparse trustworthy global matches from locally consistent motion—an idea that can be evaluated offline before any aircraft integration.

**Limits/resources:** evidence is dataset-specific and the abstract does not establish aerial-camera performance, onboard compute, long-term seasonal change or robustness when no valid anchor exists. Recall thresholds do not capture all false-positive consequences. No official code or packaged model was verified from the primary record; the HILTI26 dataset is an external dependency.

## 4. Low-bandwidth formation path following with vector fields

[Paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03016) — posted **2 November 2026**.  
Status: conference paper with journal reference to **ICUAS 2026**; independent replication was not verified.

**Demonstrated/author report:** the decentralized method superposes vector fields for path progression, path convergence, formation coordination and local separation. Each agent exchanges only one scalar coordination value. The paper reports partial convergence analysis, a formal collision-avoidance guarantee, and physical experiments with five quadrotors performing a helical V-formation and a rotating circular formation along a vertical line.

**Why it matters for AER:** it offers a transparent alternative to large learned swarm policies and exposes communication bandwidth as a measurable design variable.

**Limits/resources:** convergence is partial and excludes the separation term in the stated result; physical experiments cover two choreographies, not arbitrary clutter, link loss or heterogeneous vehicles. “Collision avoidance guaranteed” applies to the paper's assumptions, not all real flight conditions. No official source code, firmware configuration or communication stack was verified from the arXiv record.

These are AER engineering inferences, not reproductions of the papers. A phone magnetometer cannot reproduce a calibrated nine-sensor MISLAM array. Effort labels indicate relative complexity, not time promises. Any later vehicle test should follow offline replay, SITL, bench testing and an independent safety review.

## One priority

**Add a deadline-and-fallback contract to the controller benchmark before adding a second controller.** The immediate deliverable is a deterministic Python report that joins tracking metrics with command age, delay distribution, missed deadlines, fallback state and terminal outcome. This directly extends the 30 October priority and creates evidence that later PX4/ArduPilot SITL experiments can reuse.

A sensible first milestone is one PID baseline across fixed seeds and four delay bands, with explicit pass/fail criteria and no hardware dependency.

## Watchlist

- MISLAM code/data, array calibration procedure, power/weight and sensitivity to motors or high-current wiring.
- Vertical-drift mitigation and real aerial validation for magnetic-inertial localization.
- Sparse-anchor failure detection when no valid cross-session anchor exists.
- HILTI26 access details, compute requirements and reproducible evaluation scripts.
- Formation behavior under link delay/dropout, heterogeneous dynamics and uncertain relative localization.
- Whether AER recorder timestamps are accurate enough to parameterize delay and multi-session experiments.
